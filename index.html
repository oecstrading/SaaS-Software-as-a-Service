EVIDENCE & COMMERCIAL READINESS

A portfolio with a clear path forward.

This is a technical and commercial screening of all 47 listings (42 in the repository plus five recovered from browser-saved additions), dated 10 November 2026. No application has been certified production-ready by this review. Two sample workflows were exercised in the browser; other assessments use the available interface, public application bundle, matching repository or link evidence.

01 / ROUTING

15 original launch paths were missing.

Every original /apps/ link returned 404. Actual destinations were recovered where the edition could be matched; unsupported launches are now replaced with a demonstration request.

02 / PRODUCT POSITIONING

A demo is a useful starting point.

Browser prototypes and engineering proposals have value, but they need different offers from hosted operational software. Similar deployments and reporting modules should not be sold as independent engines without evidence.

03 / COMMERCIAL FOCUS

Prove one workflow, then expand.

FaMaaS, QuickBooks Cleanup, Audit Studio and payroll are focus candidates based on visible workflows and business use. This is a prioritization judgment, not a valuation or a production endorsement.

Review method and limits

Original links were checked using GET requests. The 27 company-domain URLs all served a common web-app shell, so HTTP 200 was not treated as proof of an app. The company portfolio supplied actual destinations. Matching public source was inspected for several GitHub demonstrations; public bundles and rendered content were inspected for other destinations. FaMaaS demo setup and QuickBooks Cleanup sample/validation workflows were exercised. An existing authenticated payroll interface was observed without running payroll or changing business records.

Live payments, QuickBooks writes, external notifications, full statutory correctness, tenant isolation, disaster recovery and complete end-to-end workflows were not tested. Authentication-only access and HTTP 403 are recorded as limits, not proof of failure. No private payroll or clinical records are included. Original feature descriptions and technology stacks were claims, not verified specifications.

“Demo on request” means the exact advertised source or edition was not established. Related material, when available, is labelled separately. Missing evidence is not a finding that an application cannot work.

Recommended delivery sequence

  1. First 30 days: recover missing editions, consolidate aliases and commission synthetic-data acceptance tests for the four focus candidates.
  2. Days 31–60: prove persistent records, server-side company isolation, integration failure handling, backups and reconciled accounting outputs.
  3. Days 61–90: run a bounded customer pilot, measure support cost and verified time savings, then price the service from observed delivery economics.

Indicative sequence only. Costs and dates require an agreed scope, access and pilot results.
